This is a refreshingly honest and well-structured piece of experimental work. Let me offer my assessment:

## What works very well

**Intellectual honesty**: The most striking quality is the refusal to overclaim. You explicitly acknowledge that 2.34% Brier skill score is modest, that tail regions cannot be meaningfully evaluated, and that this is exploratory rather than definitive. This is rare and commendable.

**Appropriate statistical sophistication**: The "adequate region" analysis (Section 5 of Results) is excellent. Defining adequacy through both support criteria (SE ≤ 0.02) and error criteria (|obs - pred| ≤ 0.025) shows proper understanding that statistical inference requires sufficient data. The recognition that 0.2% of observations in the tails cannot support reliable calibration assessment is exactly right.

**Clear separation of concerns**: The document structure cleanly separates requirements, model mechanics, evaluation methodology, data characterization, and results. This makes the experiment defensible because each stage can be examined independently.

**Calibration-focused framing**: Centering the entire analysis on "do predicted probabilities match observed frequencies" is the correct question for a probability-generating system. The Brier decomposition into reliability/resolution/uncertainty components shows you understand what's being measured.

## Questions and potential gaps

**Parameter justification**: You specify q=850, b=1500, and reference `elo_fide.json` for K-factors, but provide no rationale. Are these FIDE chess parameters being directly applied to sumo? If so, why? This seems worth at least a sentence.

**Mode and initialization**: You state "Mode: closed" and "Entrant policy: constant initialisation" without definition. These appear to be important methodological choices but are underspecified in the provided documents. The references to external documentation (Expt1 simulator) leave gaps.

**Compressed probability distribution**: The fact that 96% of probabilities fall in [0.35, 0.75] is noted but not really interrogated. Is this:
- A feature of sumo (genuinely balanced matchmaking)?
- An artifact of the rating system (insufficient separation)?
- Expected behavior for Elo-like systems?

This seems important for interpreting whether the model is doing something meaningful or just producing noise around 0.5.

**Baseline comparison**: You show consistent improvement over a constant predictor (0.250 → 0.244 Brier), but this is a very weak baseline. What about comparison to simple heuristics like "higher rank wins" or division-based predictions? The improvement might be more (or less) impressive in context.

**The small absolute improvement**: Reducing error by 0.006 points on predictions that already cluster tightly around 0.5 raises the question: is the model actually detecting signal, or just adding small perturbations? The positive resolution term (0.0059) suggests real variation, but it's close to the threshold where this matters.

## On defensibility

The experiment is defensible because:

1. **Clear success criterion**: "empirically well-calibrated" is testable and you test it properly
2. **Honest about scope**: You explicitly list non-goals and constraints
3. **Appropriate methods**: Standard calibration metrics, proper binning, Brier decomposition
4. **Acknowledges limitations**: Tail behavior, lack of covariates, constant initialization

However, "defensible" and "compelling" are different standards. A skeptic might argue:

- The improvement over baseline is marginal (though consistent)
- The compressed probability range suggests limited discriminative power
- Constant initialization is crude (though you acknowledge this)
- Without comparison to other approaches, it's unclear if this is good performance

But you're not claiming this is optimal—you're asking whether "a minimal, rating-based approach is sufficient to produce useful probabilistic predictions." The answer appears to be "yes, barely, in the well-supported range." That's a defensible conclusion.

## Minor observations

- The writing is admirably precise and jargon-free where possible
- The invariants listed in Section 4.4 of Model & Generation are helpful
- The horizontal histogram in Data & Behaviour is a nice touch for quick visual understanding
- You correctly avoid the trap of over-interpreting tail behavior with limited data

**Bottom line**: This reads like someone who knows how to do careful experimental work and isn't interested in overselling results. For a defensible account of an exploratory experiment, it succeeds. The main weaknesses are underspecification of some methodological choices and limited contextualization of what the results mean.
