# Proposal: Sequential Bradley–Terry (Elo-style) Model with Gaussian-Regularized MAP Updates for Sumo Ratings

## Status

Historical / deletion candidate.

This draft is early proposal material, not current Equelo implementation
guidance. Preserve only if needed for archive archaeology.

---

## 1. Overview

We propose a rating system for professional sumo that combines:

* The **Bradley–Terry / Elo win probability model**
* **Sequential (bout-by-bout) updates**
* A **Gaussian prior on rating changes**
* **Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation per bout**

The goal is to retain the interpretability and familiarity of Elo while improving:

* statistical grounding,
* stability under extreme results (e.g. 15–0),
* and adaptability across divisions.

---

## 2. Motivation

Standard Elo systems have known limitations:

* The update rule is heuristic:
  [
  R' = R + K (o - E)
  ]
* The choice of (K) is ad hoc
* Extreme results can produce unrealistic rating swings
* No explicit probabilistic framework for calibration

In sumo specifically:

* Divisions exhibit **different volatility regimes**
* Basho results are short (15 bouts), increasing noise
* Perfect or near-perfect records are common at lower levels

We seek a model that:

* respects opponent strength at the bout level,
* updates sequentially (reflecting tournament structure),
* and controls rating volatility in a principled way.

---

## 3. Model Specification

### 3.1 Win Probability

We retain the standard Elo / Bradley–Terry form:

[
P(\text{win}) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_{\text{opp}} - R)/400}}
]

---

### 3.2 Sequential Structure

Let:

* (R_{n-1}): rating before bout (n)
* (R_n): rating after bout (n)
* (R_n^{opp}): opponent rating
* (o_n \in {0,1}): outcome

Ratings evolve deterministically:

[
R_0 = R_{\text{pre-basho}}, \quad
R_n = \text{update}(R_{n-1}, R_n^{opp}, o_n)
]

---

### 3.3 MAP Update Rule

Each bout updates the rating via:

[
R_n = \arg\max_x \left[
o_n \log p(x) + (1 - o_n)\log(1 - p(x))
---------------------------------------

\frac{(x - R_{n-1})^2}{2\sigma_d^2}
\right]
]

where:

* (p(x)) is the win probability at rating (x)
* (\sigma_d) is the division-specific volatility parameter

---

## 4. Interpretation

Each update balances:

* **Likelihood**: how well a rating explains the observed result
* **Regularization**: resistance to large changes from the prior rating

This can be interpreted equivalently as:

* a **MAP estimate** with:

  * logistic likelihood (Bradley–Terry)
  * Gaussian prior centered at (R_{n-1})

or simply:

> **Elo-style update derived from an optimization problem rather than a fixed rule**

---

## 5. Role of σ (Volatility Parameter)

### 5.1 Definition

(\sigma_d) is the **standard deviation of rating changes per bout** in division (d).

It controls:

* how far a single bout can move a rating
* the effective “K-factor” of the system

---

### 5.2 Estimation

For each division (d):

1. Compute historical rating changes:
   [
   \Delta = R_{\text{after}} - R_{\text{before}}
   ]

2. Convert to per-bout scale if needed:
   [
   \sigma_{\text{bout}} \approx \frac{\sigma_{\text{basho}}}{\sqrt{N}}
   ]

3. Set:
   [
   \sigma_d = \text{std}(\Delta)
   ]

---

### 5.3 Interpretation

* Small (\sigma_d): stable division (top ranks)
* Large (\sigma_d): volatile division (lower ranks)

This naturally replaces division-specific (K)-factors.

---

## 6. Relationship to Elo

| Feature           | Elo           | Proposed Model              |
| ----------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- |
| Update rule       | Linear        | Optimization-based          |
| Parameter         | (K)           | (\sigma_d)                  |
| Per-bout updates  | Yes           | Yes                         |
| Opponent strength | Yes           | Yes                         |
| Statistical basis | Heuristic     | Likelihood + regularization |
| Extreme results   | Can overshoot | Controlled                  |

For small deviations, the update behaves similarly to:

[
R_n \approx R_{n-1} + K_{\text{eff}} (o_n - E_n)
]

but with (K_{\text{eff}}) emerging from (\sigma_d) rather than being fixed.

---

## 7. Advantages

### 7.1 Stability

* Prevents runaway ratings (e.g. undefeated basho)
* Smooths noisy results

### 7.2 Interpretability

* Retains Elo-style probabilities
* (\sigma_d) has a direct empirical meaning

### 7.3 Flexibility

* Naturally supports division-specific volatility
* Extendable to per-rikishi uncertainty (Glicko-style)

### 7.4 Order-aware

* Sequential updates reflect actual bout order

---

## 8. Limitations

* Requires numerical optimization per bout (though fast in practice)
* Sequential updates introduce order dependence (as in Elo)
* Opponent ratings treated as fixed within a basho

---

## 9. Extensions

* Player-specific volatility ((\sigma_i)) → Glicko-style system
* Joint estimation across all rikishi (global Bradley–Terry)
* Incorporation of rank-based priors or promotion/demotion effects
* Bayesian full posterior tracking instead of MAP

---

## 10. Summary

This model can be viewed succinctly as:

> **A sequential Elo system where updates are derived from maximizing a penalized likelihood, with division-specific volatility calibrated from historical data.**

It preserves the practical strengths of Elo while placing the system on a firmer statistical foundation and addressing known edge cases in sumo competition.

# Appendix A: Implementation Details

## A.1 Core Update Algorithm

For each rikishi, ratings are updated sequentially over bouts within a basho.

### Inputs per rikishi:

* Initial rating: (R_0)
* Opponent ratings: ({R_n^{opp}}_{n=1}^N)
* Outcomes: ({o_n}_{n=1}^N), where (o_n \in {0,1})
* Division volatility: (\sigma_d)

### Update loop:

For (n = 1, \dots, N):

[
R_n = \arg\max_x \left[
o_n \log p(x) + (1-o_n)\log(1-p(x))
-----------------------------------

\frac{(x - R_{n-1})^2}{2\sigma_d^2}
\right]
]

with

[
p(x) = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_n^{opp} - x)/400}}
]

Final rating after the basho is (R_N).

---

## A.2 Numerical Optimization

Each update is a **1D optimization problem**, which is:

* smooth
* strictly concave (well-behaved)
* fast to solve

Recommended method:

* bounded scalar minimization (`scipy.optimize.minimize_scalar`)

### Practical bounds

For numerical stability, restrict search to:

[
x \in [R_{n-1} - 6\sigma_d,; R_{n-1} + 6\sigma_d]
]

This captures essentially all plausible values.

---

## A.3 Reference Python Implementation

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import minimize_scalar


def win_probability(player_rating, opponent_rating):
    return 1 / (1 + 10 ** ((opponent_rating - player_rating) / 400))


def update_one_bout(current_rating, opponent_rating, outcome, sigma_d):
    def objective(x):
        p = win_probability(x, opponent_rating)
        p = np.clip(p, 1e-12, 1 - 1e-12)

        log_lik = outcome * np.log(p) + (1 - outcome) * np.log(1 - p)
        penalty = (x - current_rating) ** 2 / (2 * sigma_d ** 2)

        return -(log_lik - penalty)

    lower = current_rating - 6 * sigma_d
    upper = current_rating + 6 * sigma_d

    result = minimize_scalar(objective, bounds=(lower, upper), method="bounded")
    return result.x


def update_basho(initial_rating, opponent_ratings, outcomes, sigma_d):
    rating = initial_rating
    history = [rating]

    for opp, outcome in zip(opponent_ratings, outcomes):
        rating = update_one_bout(rating, opp, outcome, sigma_d)
        history.append(rating)

    return rating, history
```

---

## A.4 Computational Complexity

Per rikishi:

* (O(N)) updates per basho (typically (N = 15))
* Each update is a cheap 1D optimization

Total cost is negligible even for full historical datasets.

---

## A.5 Calibration of σ

### Step 1: Compute historical changes

For each division (d), compute:

[
\Delta = R_{\text{after}} - R_{\text{before}}
]

across historical basho.

---

### Step 2: Convert to per-bout scale

If (\sigma_{\text{basho}}) is the std dev of basho-level changes:

[
\sigma_d \approx \frac{\sigma_{\text{basho}}}{\sqrt{N}}
]

with (N \approx 15).

---

### Step 3: Validate

Check that:

* typical rating swings match observed behaviour
* extreme basho (e.g. 15–0) produce plausible changes

---

## A.6 Initialization

Initial ratings may be set using:

* historical rank → rating mapping
* or backfilled via:

  * running the model forward from a baseline
  * or solving a global Bradley–Terry fit

---

## A.7 Handling Edge Cases

### Perfect records (e.g. 15–0)

* No instability (unlike MLE)
* Rating increase capped naturally by (\sigma_d)

### Very weak opposition

* Limited rating gain (as expected)

### Sparse data (new rikishi)

* Can assign larger (\sigma_d) or separate parameter

---

## A.8 Practical Notes

* Clip probabilities to avoid (\log(0))
* Use float64 for numerical stability
* Cache opponent ratings if processing many rikishi

---

## A.9 Optional Simplification

If computational simplicity is preferred, a first-order approximation yields an Elo-like update:

[
R_n \approx R_{n-1} + K_d (o_n - E_n)
]

where (K_d) is calibrated to match (\sigma_d).

However, the full MAP update is recommended for accuracy and robustness.

---

## A.10 Reproducibility

The model is:

* deterministic
* order-dependent (as in Elo)
* reproducible given:

  * initial ratings
  * bout ordering
  * σ calibration
