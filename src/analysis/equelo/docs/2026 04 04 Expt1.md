# **Elo+ — A Closed-System Formulation of Elo Ratings (Draft, with TBDs)**

## 1. Introduction

The Elo rating system models competitive strength through pairwise interactions. Its defining feature is that ratings are updated locally and symmetrically: points gained by one competitor are lost by another.

This implies:

> **Rating mass is conserved within each interaction.**

However, when Elo is applied to systems with entry and exit (e.g. sumo), the population is not fixed. This raises the question:

> **What happens to this conservation property at the system level?**

Elo+ is an attempt to restore that property.

---

## 2. Elo as a Closed System

In a fixed population:

* ratings are exchanged only through matches
* total rating mass is conserved
* the mean rating is stable

This follows directly from the update rule.

---

## 3. Applying Elo to an Open Population

With entry and exit:

* entrants are initialised at (b)
* exiting participants leave with (r \neq b)

This introduces external flows of rating mass.

### Observed (from experiment)

* Mean rating is not constant in the open system
* Large population changes (e.g. 1989) produce visible shifts

### Conclusion

> **Mean drift occurs when Elo is applied to a non-closed population.**

(No claim is made here about long-run behaviour beyond what is observed.)

---

## 4. Restoring Conservation (Elo+)**

Elo+ enforces conservation by redistributing the deviation of departing participants.

Let:

* (r(i)) = rating of departing participant
* (\mu) = current mean
* (\delta = r(i) - \mu)
* (n) = number of remaining participants

Each remaining participant receives:

[
-\frac{\delta}{n}
]

### Observed

* The mean rating remains constant (up to numerical precision)

---

## 5. Why Uniform Redistribution?

Elo depends only on rating differences.

Uniform redistribution preserves:

* all pairwise differences
* expected scores (theoretically)
* update dynamics (theoretically)

### Status

* Preservation of differences: **implied by construction**
* Preservation of expectations and dynamics: **TBD (not empirically verified)**

---

## 6. Experimental Evidence

Two systems were implemented:

* Open (no redistribution)
* Closed (Elo+)

### Observed

* Open system:

  * mean rating varies over time
* Closed system:

  * mean rating remains constant (≈ (b))

### Observed (from diagnostics)

* Redistribution magnitude is small post-1989

### TBD

* Whether individual rating trajectories are identical up to a shift
* Whether rankings are unchanged
* Whether predictive performance is unchanged
* Distribution (not just max) of redistribution magnitudes
* Comparison of redistribution magnitude to typical bout updates

---

## 7. Interpretation

Elo+ enforces conservation of rating mass in an open population.

### Observed

* This eliminates drift in the mean

### TBD

* Whether this leaves all “meaningful” aspects of the rating system unchanged
* Whether the closed system better reflects competitive reality

---

## 8. Conclusion

Elo+ is a minimal modification that enforces conservation of rating mass under entry and exit.

### Established

* Mean invariance under Elo+
* Presence of drift in open system

### Not yet established

* Structural equivalence between open and closed systems
* Practical superiority of Elo+

## B. Potential Attack Surfaces (“Ura is 2230?!”)

Even if the system is internally consistent, objections may arise:

### 1. Absolute level objections

* “This rating is too high/low”
* Root cause:

  * anchoring at (b)
  * historical path dependence
  * absence of external calibration

**Response:** Elo determines relative strength, not absolute interpretation.

---

### 2. Era effects

* Different population sizes and strengths across eras
* Expansion events (e.g. 1989)

**Risk:** ratings may not be directly comparable across eras

---

### 3. Entry assumptions

* All entrants start at (b)

**Concern:**

* Is this realistic?
* Does it bias early trajectories?

---

### 4. Redistribution mechanism

* “Why equal redistribution?”

Even if theoretically justified, it may feel artificial.

---

### 5. Missing competitive context

* Elo ignores:

  * injuries
  * scheduling asymmetries
  * rank-based matchups

So ratings may appear “wrong” in specific cases.

---

### 6. Local anomalies

* Specific rikishi (e.g. Ura) may have ratings that:

  * conflict with intuition
  * reflect model limitations rather than errors
