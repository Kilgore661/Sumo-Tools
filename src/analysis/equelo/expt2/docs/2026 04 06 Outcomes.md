# **Expt2 — Outcomes**

## 1. Overview

Expt2 applies fixed-point iteration to estimate a basho-start initialisation rule by ordinal. The solver was run under both Variant A (whole-history) and Variant B (post-1989 calibration followed by full-history refinement).

This section records the observed outcomes. Interpretation is limited to what is directly supported by the results.

---

## 2. Convergence

### Observed

* The fixed-point iteration converges in both variants.
* For tolerance `ε = 1`, convergence is achieved in fewer than 130 iterations.
* Convergence is stable across runs with the same configuration.

### Observed (comparison of variants)

* Variant A and Variant B produce closely aligned results over the modern-era domain (post-1989).
* Differences between the two are small (typically within ±1 rating point for common chii).

### Interpretation

* The operator
  [
  T = \text{normalise} \circ \text{aggregate} \circ \text{simulate}
  ]
  appears to admit a stable fixed point under the chosen configuration.
* Agreement between variants suggests that the solution is not highly sensitive to initialisation when sufficient modern-era data is present.

### Not established

* Existence or uniqueness of the fixed point in general
* Convergence guarantees beyond empirical observation

---

## 3. Scale and range

### Observed

* The resulting ratings lie in a range approximately 1200–2400.
* The scale is comparable to conventional Elo systems.

### Interpretation

* The normalisation convention (mean anchored at ( b )) produces values in a familiar range.
* No rescaling or additional calibration was required to obtain interpretable magnitudes.

### Not established

* Whether the absolute scale has external meaning beyond relative comparison

---

## 4. Global structure of the mapping

### Observed

* The mapping from ordinal (chii) to rating is **largely monotonic** from:
  
  * Yokozuna (Y1e)
  * down through the divisions
  * to approximately Jd100

* When viewed at coarse resolution, the mapping resembles a smooth, convex (possibly exponential-like) curve.

### Interpretation

* The method recovers a strong global relationship between rank and rating.
* The absence of an explicit monotonicity constraint suggests that this structure is induced by the data and the Elo dynamics.

### Not established

* Functional form of the curve
* Whether the apparent convexity has a theoretical basis or is an artefact of the system

---

## 5. Local deviations from monotonicity

While the global structure is smooth, several local deviations are observed.

### 5.1 Lower-division behaviour (Jd and below)

#### Observed

* The curve is smooth down to approximately Jd100.

* Beyond this point:
  
  * the curve flattens and bends outward
  * behaviour becomes less regular

* An upward movement is observed around the Jd100 region.

* The curve then drops again near the transition to Jd1e and continues into the Jk ranks with further curvature.

#### Observed (data context)

* The depth of the Jd division varies historically (e.g. up to Jd200 in some periods).
* Lower ranks are not uniformly represented across time.

#### Interpretation

* The behaviour in sparsely populated regions may reflect:
  
  * limited sample sizes
  * historical variation in division depth

* The specific shape (including the upward movement near Jd100) is not explained by these factors alone.

#### Not established

* A causal explanation for the observed curvature
* Whether the behaviour is stable under alternative aggregation or slicing choices

---

### 5.2 Jonokuchi (Jk) region

#### Observed

* The curve exhibits an upward movement near Jk1.
* Behaviour in the Jk range is less regular than in higher divisions.

#### Interpretation (hypothesis)

* This may be related to high entrant turnover and short careers in the lowest division.

#### Status

* This is speculative.
* No direct evidence has been established within this experiment.

---

### 5.3 Makuuchi (Maegashira) region

#### Observed

* Within the Makuuchi ranks, the curve is not strictly monotonic.
* A small upward movement is observed around M13.
* In particular, some lower Maegashira ranks (e.g. M16) may have higher estimated ratings than higher ones (e.g. M12).

#### Interpretation (hypothesis)

* Possible contributing factors include:
  
  * boundary effects near promotion/relegation thresholds
  * differences in cohort composition at different ranks
  * schedule or opponent-strength effects

#### Status

* These explanations are plausible but not verified.
* The observed non-monotonicity is stable under the fixed-point solution and is not attributable to numerical noise.

---

### 5.4 Rare ranks

#### Observed

* Very rare ordinals (e.g. O3e) exhibit irregular values.
* These correspond to ranks with very few observations.

#### Interpretation

* Behaviour in these cases is dominated by small-sample effects.

---

## 6. Stability of the solution

### Observed

* The fixed-point solution is stable across:
  
  * different initialisation paths (Variants A and B)
  * repeated runs with the same configuration

* Local irregularities (e.g. Makuuchi bump, Jd curvature) persist across runs.

### Interpretation

* These features are properties of the fixed point rather than transient artefacts of iteration.

---

## 7. Summary of findings

### Established

* The fixed-point procedure converges reliably under the chosen configuration.

* A stable mapping from ordinal to rating is obtained.

* The mapping is:
  
  * smooth at coarse resolution
  * largely monotonic over the main populated domain

* Results are consistent across whole-history and post-1989-calibrated variants.

### Observed but not explained

* Curvature changes in the lower divisions (especially beyond Jd100)
* Upward movement near Jd100
* Irregular behaviour in the Jk range
* Local non-monotonicity within Makuuchi (e.g. around M13)

### Not established

* Whether the fixed point is unique
* Whether the mapping is robust to alternative modelling choices
* Whether the resulting initialisation improves downstream rating behaviour

---

## 8. Position

Expt2 demonstrates that a self-consistent basho-start initialisation rule by ordinal can be obtained via fixed-point iteration. The resulting mapping exhibits a strong global structure consistent with rank ordering, together with local deviations whose causes are not yet established.

Further investigation is required to determine whether these deviations reflect:

* genuine structural features of the system, or
* artefacts of modelling choices and data limitations.
