# **Elo+ — A Closed-System Formulation of Elo Ratings (Draft, with TBDs)**

## 1. Introduction

The Elo rating system models competitive strength through pairwise interactions. Its defining feature is that ratings are updated locally and symmetrically: points gained by one competitor are lost by another.

This implies:

> **Rating mass is conserved within each interaction.**

However, when Elo is applied to systems with entry and exit (e.g. sumo), the population is not fixed. This raises the question:

> **What happens to this conservation property at the system level?**

Elo+ is an attempt to restore that property.

---

## 2. Elo as a Closed System

In a fixed population:

* ratings are exchanged only through matches
* total rating mass is conserved
* the mean rating is stable

This follows directly from the update rule.

---

## 3. Applying Elo to an Open Population

With entry and exit:

* entrants are initialised at (b)
* exiting participants leave with (r \neq b)

This introduces external flows of rating mass.

### Observed (from experiment)

* Mean rating is not constant in the open system
* Large population changes (e.g. 1989) produce visible shifts

### Conclusion

> **Mean drift occurs when Elo is applied to a non-closed population.**

(No claim is made here about long-run behaviour beyond what is observed.)

---

## 4. Restoring Conservation (Elo+)**

Elo+ enforces conservation by redistributing the deviation of departing participants.

Let:

* (r(i)) = rating of departing participant
* (\mu) = current mean
* (\delta = r(i) - \mu)
* (n) = number of remaining participants

Each remaining participant receives:

[
-\frac{\delta}{n}
]

### Observed

* The mean rating remains constant (up to numerical precision)

---

## 5. Why Uniform Redistribution?

Elo depends only on rating differences.

Uniform redistribution preserves:

* all pairwise differences
* expected scores (theoretically)
* update dynamics (theoretically)

### Status

* Preservation of differences: **implied by construction**
* Preservation of expectations and dynamics: **TBD (not empirically verified)**

---

## 6. Experimental Evidence

Two systems were implemented:

* Open (no redistribution)
* Closed (Elo+)

### Observed

* Open system:
  
  * mean rating varies over time

* Closed system:
  
  * mean rating remains constant (≈ (b))

### Observed (from diagnostics)

* Redistribution magnitude is small post-1989

### TBD

* Whether individual rating trajectories are identical up to a shift
* Whether rankings are unchanged
* Whether predictive performance is unchanged
* Distribution (not just max) of redistribution magnitudes
* Comparison of redistribution magnitude to typical bout updates

---

## 7. Interpretation

Elo+ enforces conservation of rating mass in an open population.

### Observed

* This eliminates drift in the mean

### TBD

* Whether this leaves all “meaningful” aspects of the rating system unchanged
* Whether the closed system better reflects competitive reality

---

## 8. Conclusion

Elo+ is a minimal modification that enforces conservation of rating mass under entry and exit.

### Established

* Mean invariance under Elo+
* Presence of drift in open system

### Not yet established

* Structural equivalence between open and closed systems
* Practical superiority of Elo+

## B. Potential Attack Surfaces (“Ura is 2230?!”)

Even if the system is internally consistent, objections may arise:

### 1. Absolute level objections

* “This rating is too high/low”

* Root cause:
  
  * anchoring at (b)
  * historical path dependence
  * absence of external calibration

**Response:** Elo determines relative strength, not absolute interpretation.

---

### 2. Era effects

* Different population sizes and strengths across eras
* Expansion events (e.g. 1989)

**Risk:** ratings may not be directly comparable across eras

---

### 3. Entry assumptions

* All entrants start at (b)

**Concern:**

* Is this realistic?
* Does it bias early trajectories?

---

### 4. Redistribution mechanism

* “Why equal redistribution?”

Even if theoretically justified, it may feel artificial.

---

### 5. Missing competitive context

* Elo ignores:
  
  * injuries
  * scheduling asymmetries
  * rank-based matchups

So ratings may appear “wrong” in specific cases.

---

### 6. Local anomalies

* Specific rikishi may have ratings that:
  
  * conflict with intuition
  * reflect model limitations rather than errors

# **Appendix — Running Expt1**

## 1. Command-line entry point

Expt1 is executed via the package entry point:

```bash
python -m src.analysis.equelo.expt1
```

Optional flags:

- `--closed`  
  Use closed-system (Elo+) semantics. Departures are redistributed so that the active-population mean is preserved.

- `--start <year>`  
  First year of history slice (default: project `EPOCH`)

- `--end <year>`  
  Final year of history slice (default: current year)

- `--zip`  
  Load data from local archive instead of live store

Example (Elo+ run over full history):

```bash
python -m src.analysis.equelo.expt1 --closed
```

---

## 2. What the run does

A run performs:

1. Load raw historical data

2. Apply cleansing rules (observability policy, annotation collapse)

3. Construct the simulation “oracle”

4. Run the Elo simulation over all basho in chronological order

5. Collect diagnostics during the run

6. Print a summary to console and write diagnostics to disk

---

## 3. Console output

A typical run prints:

- number of basho processed

- number of rikishi in the final basho

- a sample rating lookup

- diagnostic summary including:
  
  - max deviation of mean rating from baseline ( b )
  
  - rating-mass conservation error
  
  - counts of entries, retirements
  
  - counts of ignored bouts (`fusen`, `blank`)
  
  - output file paths

These values are sufficient to verify the core invariants:

- **open mode**: mean may drift

- **closed mode (Elo+)**: mean remains constant up to numerical precision

---

## 4. Output files

All outputs are written under:

```text
files/output/Equelo/
```

For a given run (suffix depends on mode), the following files are produced:

- `basho_summary_<mode>.csv`  
  Per-basho summary:
  
  - number of rikishi
  
  - total rating mass
  
  - mean rating
  
  - mean absolute bout update

- `retirements_<mode>.csv`  
  One row per retirement event:
  
  - rating at exit
  
  - deviation from mean
  
  - redistribution applied (closed mode)

- `run_log_<mode>.txt`  
  Text summary of key diagnostics:
  
  - conservation checks
  
  - counts of events
  
  - redistribution magnitudes (pre/post 1989)

# **Appendix — Interpreting the Output**

## 1. `basho_summary_<mode>.csv`

Each row corresponds to one basho (tournament).

Columns:

- `date`  
  Basho identifier (e.g. `2026/03`)

- `n_rikishi`  
  Number of active rikishi on the banzuke for that basho

- `rating_mass`  
  Sum of ratings over the active population

- `mean_rating`  
  Average rating over the active population

- `mean_abs_bout_update`  
  Average absolute Elo update per scored bout in that basho

### How to read it

- **Mean behaviour**
  
  - Open mode: `mean_rating` may drift over time
  
  - Closed mode (Elo+): `mean_rating ≈ b` throughout

- **Conservation check**
  
  - `rating_mass` should be stable in closed mode (up to float noise)
  
  - Differences between consecutive basho reflect entry/exit handling

- **Scale of updates**
  
  - `mean_abs_bout_update` gives a reference scale for comparison with redistribution magnitudes

---

## 2. `retirements_<mode>.csv`

Each row corresponds to one retirement (departure) event.

Columns:

- `date`  
  Basho in which the retirement occurs

- `rikid`  
  Rikishi identifier

- `rating`  
  Rating at the point of exit

- `n`  
  Number of surviving rikishi over which any redistribution is applied

- `delta`  
  Difference between the retiree’s rating and the active mean

- `delta_per_rikishi`  
  Amount added to (or subtracted from) each survivor

- `abs_delta_per_rikishi`  
  Absolute magnitude of that adjustment

### How to read it

- **Open mode**
  
  - `delta_per_rikishi = 0` (no redistribution)

- **Closed mode (Elo+)**
  
  - Each retirement induces a uniform adjustment to all survivors
  
  - Sign:
    
    - positive → survivors gain rating
    
    - negative → survivors lose rating

- **Key diagnostic**
  
  - Compare `abs_delta_per_rikishi` to `mean_abs_bout_update` (from basho summary)
  
  - If much smaller, redistribution is negligible relative to normal Elo updates

---

## 3. `run_log_<mode>.txt`

A compact textual summary of the run.

Includes:

- baseline parameters (`b`, `q`)

- maximum deviation of mean rating from ( b )

- maximum rating-mass change across bouts

- counts:
  
  - entries
  
  - retirements
  
  - ignored bouts (`fusen`, `blank`)

- maximum redistribution per rikishi:
  
  - pre-1989
  
  - post-1989

### How to read it

- **Sanity checks**
  
  - `max abs rating-mass change across scored bout ≈ 0`  
    → verifies zero-sum updates

- **Mean invariance (closed mode)**
  
  - `max abs basho-end mean deviation from b` should be very small

- **Redistribution scale**
  
  - Compare pre- vs post-1989 values to assess impact of the observability cliff

---

## 4. What matters most

For the purposes of Expt1, the key quantities are:

1. **Mean rating over time** (`basho_summary`)

2. **Magnitude of redistribution** (`retirements`)

3. **Conservation checks** (`run_log`)

Together these establish:

- whether the system exhibits drift (open)

- whether Elo+ removes it (closed)

- whether the correction is large enough to matter

---

## 5. What is *not* shown here

These outputs do **not** directly show:

- individual rating trajectories

- ranking stability

- predictive performance

Those require additional queries on the in-memory rating results or further analysis.
