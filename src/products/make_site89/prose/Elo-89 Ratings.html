What is Elo-89?

Elo-89 is the site's rating model for the sufficiently complete sumo record from January 1989 onward. It applies ordinary Elo-style updates to represented binary win/loss results: beating a more highly rated opponent is worth more, and losing to a lower-rated opponent costs more.

The model uses a fixed chii-based entrant-rating table called P1, a rating scale parameter of 400, and a divisional K schedule. P1 supplies a plausible starting rating for a rikishi entering the represented population at a given banzuke rank.

Sumo's population changes continually. Elo-89 therefore restores the active population to one fixed mean after the population has been formed for a basho and again after the basho's results have been processed. Each restoration is a common additive shift, so it preserves the rating differences—and therefore the forecast probabilities—among the active rikishi at that instant.

When a rikishi leaves the represented population, his rating is discarded. If he later returns, he receives the P1 entrant rating associated with his current chii. Ratings shown by this site are outputs of this particular model, not direct measurements of an unobservable true ability.

Elo-89 was selected by retrospective comparison with the other tested post-1988 Elo-family models. Its P1 values were constructed using the same historical period, so those comparisons describe retrospective performance; they are not claims of prospective validation.
