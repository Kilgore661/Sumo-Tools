This page collects the main assumptions behind Equelo in one place. The public explanation describes these points in context; this list is intended as a more auditable checklist of the modelling choices that shape the ratings.

Assumption 1: the banzuke is evidence

A chii is not a perfect measure of strength, and the banzuke is not a rating table. But a rank is not arbitrary. It reflects previous results, institutional judgement, and the constraints of the ranking system. Equelo uses that information, especially where the underlying bout record is incomplete.

Assumption 2: fixed-point stability is meaningful within the model

Equelo estimates chii-based initial ratings by iteration. If using a chii rating map as input reproduces approximately the same map as output, the resulting values are treated as self-consistent estimates. This does not make them true in an absolute sense. It means they are stable under the defined process.

Assumption 3: the active rating mean should be conserved

Equelo assumes that changes in the size and composition of the active population should not move the meaning of the rating scale. Mean conservation is a modelling choice, justified by the need to distinguish performance changes from rating-mass changes caused by entry and retirement.

Assumption 4: later complete data can calibrate earlier incomplete data

Complete lower-division bout records are only available from January 1989. Equelo uses the better-recorded period to estimate rank-rating relationships across the full banzuke, then applies those relationships cautiously to earlier periods where the banzuke exists but lower-division bout records are incomplete.

Assumption 5: unsupported chii should be completed, not independently estimated

Some chii have too little support to estimate safely on their own. Equelo uses a defined completion rule for poorly supported chii rather than preserving raw estimates that look precise but are not disciplined by enough data.

Assumption 6: rating differences carry the predictive meaning

The absolute numbers are placed on a scale by convention. The comparisons made by the system are driven by differences between ratings. A rating should therefore be read as model-derived comparative evidence, not as an absolute measure of ability.

Assumption 7: lower-division churn does not by itself invalidate the rating process

Equelo applies Elo-like dynamics throughout the banzuke, including regions with high turnover. This assumption is least secure in Jonokuchi and low Jonidan. Those ranks remain included because they are part of the banzuke and part of the career path of rikishi who later matter to the upper divisions, but claims based on those regions should be weaker.
